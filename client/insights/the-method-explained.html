Step 1 — Diagnose: Going Deep Before Going Wide

The diagnosis phase is where most consultants rush. They ask a few surface questions, confirm what the client already believes, and move straight to recommendations. We do not do that.

The Diagnose step is designed to uncover what is actually blocking growth. That means examining the offer, the positioning, the page structure, the messaging, the conversion path, and the competitive context — without assumptions.

Common findings at this stage:

  • An offer that is technically strong but communicated poorly
  • A website that looks professional but has no clear conversion logic
  • Ad spend driving traffic to a page that cannot convert
  • Messaging that speaks to the business owner instead of the customer
  • No clear differentiation from competitors in the same space

As Nielsen Norman Group's foundational UX research has consistently shown, clarity and usability problems are almost always invisible to those closest to the product. An outside diagnostic view changes everything.

Step 2 — Design: Strategy Without a Template

Once the diagnosis is complete, we build the strategy. Not a generic one assembled from previous clients. One designed specifically for your business, your market, and your goals.

The Design step typically covers:

  • Offer clarity and positioning refinement
  • Messaging angles and copy direction
  • Landing page structure and visual hierarchy
  • Funnel logic — what happens before and after the page
  • Ad creative direction for Meta and Google campaigns
  • A prioritised 30-day action plan

The output is not a report to be filed away. It is a working document — a strategic foundation every piece of execution builds on.

Step 3 — Deploy: Where Strategy Becomes Real

Deploy is where the strategy stops being a document and starts being a system.

The gap between a good strategy and a good outcome is almost always execution. Businesses get stuck. Pages do not get built. Copy does not get written. Campaigns do not get launched.

Deploy is the step where we close that gap. Depending on the engagement, this means building the landing page, writing the copy, structuring the ads, setting up tracking, and aligning every element so it works as a system — not a collection of isolated tasks.

This is especially relevant for businesses running Meta or Google ad campaigns, where the ad and the page must work together or neither works at all.

Step 4 — Deliver: Results That Last

Delivery is not just hitting a launch date. It is the ongoing responsibility to ensure the work produces real results.

The Deliver step includes reviewing performance, refining based on data, and supporting iteration so the system improves over time. Most engagements include a 14-day support period specifically for this — because a launch is not the finish line.

We measure. We refine. We stand behind the outcome. That is what separates a strategic partner from a vendor.

Why The Method Works

The framework works because it does not skip steps. Most marketing failures come from deploying tactics without a clear strategy, or designing a strategy without an honest diagnosis. The Method forces every engagement to move in order — and that discipline creates consistency in results.

It works because it is built around the real commercial question every business needs to answer: not "how do we get more followers" but "how do we turn attention into enquiries, and enquiries into clients."

That is a fundamentally different question — and it leads to fundamentally different work.
